Seven Roads in One Week

Road Trip Multi-Day Itinerary New Zealand

In just one week on New Zealand’s South Island, you’ll experience such a dramatic variety of landscapes that it often feels as though you’ve crossed the borders of several different countries. Your day may begin on vast agricultural plains stretching to the horizon, only for you to find yourself standing beside a brilliantly turquoise glacial lake a few hours later. The next morning brings the country’s highest mountain, followed by golden high-country hills, and before long you’re driving through rainforest, granite cliffs, and waterfalls on your way to a fjord.

That is what makes this one of the best road trips on the South Island. The route has been designed so that every day feels completely different from the last. Rather than simply travelling between attractions, you gradually discover the island’s many different personalities.

Day 1. Christchurch → Lake Tekapo

Your journey begins in Christchurch, the South Island’s largest city. Within a couple of hours, the urban landscape gives way to the turquoise lakes of the Southern Alps. There’s no need to rush on the first day. The best approach is to settle into the rhythm of the journey and enjoy the drive.

Spend the afternoon exploring Lake Tekapo. Walk along the lakeshore, visit the Church of the Good Shepherd, and stay for sunset if you can. On a clear evening, wait until darkness falls. The lake lies within the Aoraki Mackenzie International Dark Sky Reserve, making it one of the world’s finest places for stargazing.

Day 2. Lake Tekapo → Lake Pukaki → Aoraki / Mount Cook

For many travellers, this is the highlight of the entire journey. The road follows the shores of Lake Pukaki before joining the famous SH80, which gradually leads you toward Aoraki / Mount Cook, New Zealand’s highest mountain.

Take your time before reaching the national park. Stop at Peter’s Lookout, then choose one of the short walks along the way. Kea Point and the Tasman Glacier View are excellent options, offering spectacular alpine scenery without requiring a full-day hike.

If your schedule allows, spend the night near Mount Cook. Once the day visitors leave, the national park becomes remarkably peaceful.

Day 3. Aoraki / Mount Cook → Lindis Pass → Wānaka

The scenery changes dramatically this morning. Snow-covered peaks slowly disappear behind you as Lindis Pass introduces an entirely different landscape.

The forests fade away, replaced by golden tussock-covered hills, wide valleys, and open country that looks unlike anywhere you’ve seen so far. It is hard to believe these landscapes are only a few hours apart.

By evening, you’ll arrive in Wānaka. Smaller and quieter than Queenstown, it is an ideal place to slow down after another unforgettable day on the road.

Day 4. Wānaka

After three days of driving, it’s worth taking a break.

Wānaka isn’t a town that needs to be rushed. Its greatest appeal lies in its atmosphere. Hike to one of the surrounding viewpoints, walk along the lakefront, spend the day on a scenic trail, or simply enjoy a long lunch at a local café. A slower day feels perfectly timed before the journey continues.

Day 5. Wānaka → Queenstown

There are two ways to reach Queenstown, but if the weather is good, choose Crown Range.

As New Zealand’s highest sealed mountain road, it deserves to be part of the journey. The climb rewards you with sweeping views across Central Otago, and you’ll probably find yourself stopping at several lookouts before reaching the summit.

By evening, you’ll arrive in Queenstown, the South Island’s adventure capital. Stroll along the waterfront, ride the Skyline Gondola, or simply enjoy the lively atmosphere of the town.

Day 6. Queenstown → Glenorchy

This day is almost entirely about the drive itself.

Although Glenorchy is less than an hour from Queenstown, there is no reason to hurry. The road follows the shores of Lake Wakatipu almost the entire way, with the scenery changing around nearly every corner. Bennett’s Bluff is an essential stop, but don’t limit yourself to the famous viewpoints. Some of the most memorable views are found at the small roadside pull-offs scattered along the route.

Once you reach Glenorchy, take a walk to the iconic wooden jetty, explore the lakeshore, or simply spend a few hours surrounded by some of New Zealand’s most spectacular scenery.

If You Have Two More Days

The perfect extension is Milford Sound.

Drive back toward Te Anau and spend the night there before setting off early the following morning along Milford Road. Don’t rush this drive. The journey itself is one of New Zealand’s greatest attractions. Mirror Lakes, the Eglinton Valley, The Chasm, and Homer Tunnel deserve just as much attention as the fjord waiting at the end.

Once you reach Milford Sound, the best way to experience it is from the water. A cruise reveals the immense scale of its granite cliffs, waterfalls, and rainforest in a way that simply isn’t possible from the shore.

Practical Tips

The best time for this road trip is from November through April, when road conditions are generally more predictable and daylight hours are longer. During the summer season, it’s worth booking accommodation near Mount Cook, Wānaka, Queenstown, and Te Anau well in advance.

Allow at least six days for this itinerary, though eight days is ideal. That extra time gives you the freedom to stop whenever something catches your eye—because on the South Island, those unplanned moments often become the most memorable part of the journey.

On a map, this route looks surprisingly straightforward. Yet over the course of a single week, it brings together almost everything that makes the South Island unforgettable: glacial lakes, alpine peaks, golden mountain passes, peaceful lakeside towns, some of the world’s most scenic roads, and dramatic fjords.

That is why so many travellers consider it the perfect first journey through New Zealand’s South Island. Not because it includes every famous attraction, but because it reveals just how astonishingly diverse this island can be.
